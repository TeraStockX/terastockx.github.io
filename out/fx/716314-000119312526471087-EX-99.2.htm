Use of Key Performance Indicators This presentation includes key performance indicators such as backlog. Management uses backlog as a measure of current and future business and financial performance, and it may not be comparable with measures provided by other companies. Backlog is defined as the total dollar value of net orders received for which revenue has not yet been recognized. Management believes tracking backlog is useful as it often times is a leading indicator of future performance. Given that backlog is an operational measure and that the Company’s methodology for calculating it does not meet the definition of a non-GAAP measure, as that term is defined by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, a quantitative reconciliation for backlog is not required or provided. Any variation between the Company’s actual results and preliminary financial estimates set forth above may be material. Adjusted EBITDA is defined as consolidated net income (loss) before net interest expense, income taxes, depreciation, amortization, other acquisition related expenses, and other unusual/nonrecurring expenses. Adjusted EBITDA is not a measure determined in accordance with GAAP. Nevertheless, Graham believes that providing Adjusted EBITDA is important for investors and other readers of Graham's financial statements, as it is used as an analytical indicator by Graham's management to better understand operating performance. Moreover, Graham’s credit facility also contains ratios based on Adjusted EBITDA. Because Adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP measure and is susceptible to varying calculations, it may not be directly comparable to other similarly titled measures used by other companies. Organic Revenue Growth is defined as the period-over-period change in net revenue after adjusting for the impact of acquisitions, divestitures, and other items that are not considered indicative of underlying operating performance. Organic Revenue Growth is not a measure defined in accordance with GAAP. Nevertheless, Graham believes that providing Organic Revenue Growth is important for investors and other readers of Graham’s financial statements, as it is used as an analytical indicator by Graham’s management to better understand revenue trends by isolating growth from its existing business operations and excluding the effects of acquisitions, divestitures, and other items that are not considered indicative of underlying operating performance. Because Organic Revenue Growth is a non-GAAP measure, it is susceptible to varying calculations and may not be directly comparable to other similarly titled measures used by other companies.
