Item 1.01 Entry into a Material Definitive Agreement.

 

Standby Equity Purchase Agreement

 

On November 23, 2026, Everli Global Holdings, Inc. (the “Company”) entered into that certain Standby Equity Purchase Agreement (the “Purchase Agreement”) with YA II PN, Ltd., a Cayman Islands exempt limited partnership (“Yorkville”).

 

Pursuant to and subject to the terms of the Purchase Agreement, the Company has the right, but not the obligation, to sell to Yorkville, and Yorkville is obligated to purchase from the Company, up to $200,000,000 of shares of the Company’s Class A common stock, $0.0001 par value per share (“Class A Common Stock”), at the Company’s request at any time and from time to time during the 36 months following the date of the Purchase Agreement. At the Company’s option, the shares would be purchased at either (i) 95% of the VWAP (as defined below) during the period commencing on the date (each date, an “Advance Notice Date”) the Company is deemed to have delivered a written notice to Yorkville setting forth the number of shares of Class A Common Stock that the Company desires to issue and sell to Yorkville in accordance with the terms of the Purchase Agreement (each notice, an “Advance Notice”) or (ii) 97% of the lowest daily VWAP during the three consecutive trading days commencing on the Advance Notice Date. In certain circumstances, the Company may also specify a minimum acceptable price per share in an Advance Notice (each issuance and sale, an “Advance”). “VWAP” means, for any trading day or specified period, the volume weighted average price of the Class A Common Stock for such date or period on the Nasdaq Global Market (“Nasdaq”) as reported by Bloomberg L.P. through its “AQR” function. While there is no mandatory minimum amount for any Advance, it may not exceed an amount equal to 100.0% of the average of the daily traded amount on the five consecutive trading days immediately preceding an Advance Notice.

 

Under applicable Nasdaq rules, in no event may Yorkville purchase shares of Class A Common Stock from the Company under the Purchase Agreement that would exceed 19.99% of the aggregate number of shares of Class A Common Stock issued and outstanding immediately prior to the execution of the Purchase Agreement (the “Exchange Cap”), unless the Company has received stockholder approval for such issuance. The Exchange Cap will not apply under certain circumstances, including, among other circumstances, if the average price of all applicable sales of Class A Common Stock under the Purchase Agreement equals or exceeds a certain minimum price. Moreover, the Company may not issue or sell any shares of Class A Common Stock to Yorkville under the Purchase Agreement which, when aggregated with all other shares of Class A Common Stock then beneficially owned by Yorkville and its affiliates (as calculated pursuant to Section 13(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended (the “Exchange Act”), and Rule 13d-3 thereunder), would result in Yorkville beneficially owning more than 4.99% of the then-outstanding voting power or number of shares of Class A Common Stock.

 

Yorkville’s obligation to purchase shares of Class A Common Stock pursuant to the Purchase Agreement is subject to a number of conditions, including that the Company file a registration statement (the “Registration Statement”) with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (the “SEC”), registering the resale of the shares to be issued pursuant to any Advance under the Securities Act of 1933, as amended (the “Securities Act”), and that the Registration Statement is declared effective by the SEC.

 

As consideration for Yorkville’s commitment to purchase the shares of Class A Common Stock pursuant the Purchase Agreement, the Company paid Yorkville a due diligence fee in the amount of $25,000 and has agreed to pay Yorkville $4,000,000 (the “Commitment Fee”) with $500,000 to be paid on the six-month anniversary of the entry into the Purchase Agreement, $500,000 to be paid on the one-year anniversary of the Purchase Agreement and $500,000 to be paid each quarter thereafter; provided that the Company will pay a portion of the Commitment Fee earlier upon receipt of proceeds from an Advance equal to 5% of such proceeds.

 

The Purchase Agreement contains customary representations, warranties, conditions and indemnification obligations of the parties. The representations, warranties and covenants contained in such agreements were made only for purposes of such agreements and as of specific dates, were solely for the benefit of the parties to such agreements and may be subject to limitations agreed upon by the contracting parties.

 

The Company has made substantial efforts over the last year to improve its operational and financial health through a combination of revenue and margin improvement plans, financial and organizational restructuring implementations, and cost reduction initiatives. In addition, the Company has been exploring ways to pay down its currently outstanding debt, which would also help to lower the Company’s cost structure, including lowering the interest expense and other fees the Company has incurred as a result of the Company’s outstanding debt. The Company believes that proceeds from the sale of shares of Class A Common Stock pursuant to the Purchase Agreement could significantly decrease and potentially remove the Company’s debt obligations and increase the Company’s ability to focus on its business strategy and the execution of its business objectives.

Convertible Note

 

On October 21, 2025, Everli Global Inc., our wholly-owned subsidiary, issued a Secured Promissory Note and Pledge Agreement (as amended, the “Convertible Note”) in the aggregate principal amount of $7,500,000, including $750,000 of original issue discount, to Melar Capital Group LLC (“MCG”), an affiliate of the sponsor of our company prior to the Business Combination. We amended the note on November 20, 2026, and subsequent to the amendment but prior to the Business Combination, MCG sold the Convertible Note to an unaffiliated third party. In connection with the amendment, our CEO agreed to transfer shares of Everli Global Inc. Class A common stock to the new holder that were exchanged for 300,000 of our shares of Class A common stock upon the conclusion of the Business Combination. As of the date hereof, approximately, $8,904,169 of principal and interest is due on the Convertible Note.

 

Until the earliest of (A) January 13, 2027, (B) the date the applicable registration statement is declared effective and (C) the occurrence of an event of default (such date, the “Variable Price Date”), any holder of the Convertible Note may convert any portion of the outstanding loan and accrued interest into shares of our Class A common stock at a conversion price equal to $11.00 (the “Fixed Conversion Price”), as may be adjusted for stock splits and similar events and for certain equity issuances below the Fixed Conversion Price. On and after the Variable Price Date, any portion of the outstanding loan and accrued interest on the Convertible Note may be converted into shares of our Class A common stock at a conversion price equal to the lower of (i) the Fixed Conversion Price and (ii) 95% of the lowest daily volume-weighted average price (“VWAP”) during the ten trading days before such conversion (the “Variable Price”). From and after an Event of Default, the Variable Price is 80% of the lowest daily VWAP during the twenty Trading Days before the conversion date; provided, however, in no event shall the Variable Price be lower than 20% of the lower of (x) the Nasdaq official closing price of the Class A common stock on the trading day immediately before the Variable Price Date and (y) the average Nasdaq official closing price of the Class A common stock for the five trading days immediately before the Variable Price Date (the “Floor Price”).

 

No conversion may be made under the Convertible Note if doing so would cause the person converting the Convertible Note, together with any affiliates, to beneficially own more than 4.99% of our then outstanding shares of Class A common stock. Additionally, we may not issue shares of Class A common stock that exceed 19.99% of the shares of Class A Common Stock outstanding on the measurement date that Nasdaq applies to the Note under Rule 5635(d) unless we have obtained shareholder approval for such issuances. We have agreed to seek shareholder approval for this matter.

 

We have agreed that if the Convertible Note is not repaid in full by January 31, 2027, we will issue the holder an additional 500,000 shares of Class A common stock.

 

The Convertible Note bears interest at 17.5% per annum, payable quarterly and calculated on the basis of actual days elapsed and a 365-day year, and matures on January 31, 2027.

 

We have agreed to register for resale with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, the 500,000 shares to be issued if the note is not repaid in full as well as shares underlying the conversion of the Convertible Note.

Item 3.02 Unregistered Sales of Equity Securities.

 

The information contained above in Item 1.01 is incorporated herein by reference.

 

On March 6, 2026, Everli entered into a Note Purchase Agreement (the “NPA”) with Yorkville, pursuant to which Yorkville agreed to provide Everli with up to $10 million of financing in the form of convertible promissory notes to be advanced in multiple tranches. Prior to November 23, 2026, Yorkville advanced funds to Everli Global, Inc., a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Company (the “Wholly-Owned Subsidiary”) in exchange for four convertible notes with an aggregate principal amount of $8 million. On November 23, 2026, Yorkville advanced the final tranche of $2 million to the Wholly-Owned Subsidiary in exchange for a fifth convertible note under the NPA (the “Fifth Convertible Note”).

 

The Fifth Convertible Note bears interest at 8% per annum, which increases to 18% upon an event of default, and have a maturity of September 6, 2027. The notes are convertible into shares of the Company’s Class A Common Stock, subject to the terms and limitations set forth in the notes. The obligations under the notes are guaranteed by certain subsidiaries of the Company pursuant to a global guaranty agreement.

 

Upon the acquisition by the Company of the Wholly-Owned Subsidiary on November 23, 2026, the Wholly-Owned Subsidiary assigned to the Company all rights and obligations under the NPA, the notes issued pursuant to the NPA and all related agreements, and the Company is now considered the maker of the notes issued under the NPA.

 

In the Purchase Agreement, Yorkville represented to the Company, among other things, that it is an “accredited investor” (as such term is defined in Rule 501(a) of Regulation D under the Securities Act). The Fifth Convertible Note and the securities being issued pursuant to the Purchase Agreement and the Fifth Convertible Note are being issued and sold by the Company to Yorkville in reliance upon the exemptions from the registration requirements of the Securities Act afforded by Section 4(a)(2) of the Securities Act. The Company is relying on this exemption from registration in part on representations made by Yorkville in the Purchase Agreement.
